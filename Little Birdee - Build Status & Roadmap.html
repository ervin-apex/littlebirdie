The roadmap

Built in this order because each stage depends on the one before it. Skipping ahead means redoing work later.

  1. Finish the financial foundationMove every screen onto the tested profit engine, and prove the P&L rules against your five samples. An accountant should sanity-check the classifications while we build.
  2. Accounts, businesses & venuesSign-up, login, and secure storage — so your numbers live in a real database, not one browser, and multi-venue owners keep them separate. Has to come before any P&L is uploaded.
  3. P&L upload & AI extractionUpload a P&L from any accounting system; the AI pulls out your COGS percentage and amortised fixed/variable costs, then shows them to you to confirm before anything goes live.
  4. Weekly plan & real daily numbersPredicted revenue and labour in, actual daily revenue back. First proper week a real operator can run without demo data.
  5. Reporting on live dataThe approved screens wired to real records, with proper handling for missing days, plus month and custom-range trends.
  6. Privacy, compliance & billingPrivacy policy, terms, $12/week subscription, no lock-in, cancel anytime. Required before taking a single payment.
  7. Daily ChirpsScheduled email at a time each operator picks, linking straight to Yesterday. Last, because a Chirp is only worth sending once the numbers behind it are real.

Where AI speeds things up — and where it doesn't

The estimates above bake in a solid discount for AI-assisted development, but it isn't even across the board. Accounts, billing, settings, the test suites and the website build all move considerably faster because they're well-trodden ground.

The P&L extraction engine is the exception. The hard part there isn't writing the code, it's iterating until it reliably reads the right numbers out of five different accountants' layouts — and then checking it got them right. That's judgement and testing, which doesn't compress much. Same goes for verifying the profit maths: on a product whose whole promise is "this is your profit," being certain the number is correct is the job, not the overhead.

A faster path, if you want something live sooner

Stages 1, 2, 4 and 5 alone (roughly 45–63 hours) get you a genuinely usable product with manual number entry — real accounts, real data, real reporting. The P&L upload engine could follow straight after. Worth deciding whether you'd rather have real operators using a simpler version early, or wait for the full experience.

Waiting on you

  • An accountant to check the P&L rules. Mainly whether labour includes superannuation and other on-costs. This affects every profit number, so it's worth getting right once.
  • Daily labour — how do we get it? You noted operators know daily revenue but rarely daily labour. Options: enter it manually, upload a roster summary, or spread the weekly figure across days. My lean is spreading the weekly figure to start, with Deputy replacing it as soon as we can.
  • Domain purchase — for when the website goes up.
  • Launch order. The full build, or the faster manual-entry path above.

One thing to keep in mind

The P&L upload engine (stage 3) is the most valuable and least predictable piece of the build. Reading a COGS percentage reliably out of five different accountants' layouts is genuinely hard, and it must always show its working for you to confirm rather than silently commit a number. I've built in a confirmation step for exactly this reason — but it's the stage most likely to move on time.

Parked for later

Direct Xero and MYOB integrations (the P&L upload covers all of them in one go), POS connections until after Deputy, phone push notifications until email Chirps are proven, and anything resembling a general accounting or tax engine.
